# Where to Share StatFlowers

Saved for the first public preview and later updates.

## Suggested order

1. **[r/generative](https://www.reddit.com/r/generative/)** — lead with an overhead sunflower spiral and show 137.5° versus other divergence angles. Emphasize generative rules and the visuals.
2. **[r/threejs](https://www.reddit.com/r/threejs/)** — show a short 3D flower/bloom recording. Explain the parametric petal surfaces and ask for specific geometry and lighting feedback.
3. **[r/proceduralgeneration](https://www.reddit.com/r/proceduralgeneration/)** — share how different local parameters lead to different flower families. Focus on reproducibility and algorithms.
4. **[itch.io](https://itch.io/)** — distribute the playable browser experiment; provide clear controls and credit the project as a prototype.
5. **[Show HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html)** — consider once the main mathematical and biological mechanisms are documented and there is a polished demo.
6. **Three.js Discourse / creative-coding communities** — ask for targeted review of petal geometry, rendering, and the tradeoff between mathematical patterns and botanical realism.

Check each community's current self-promotion rules before posting. Avoid posting the same promotional message in several communities simultaneously.

## Suggested 10–15-second launch clip

- **Opening:** Sunflower top view; adjust divergence angle from 137.5° to 140° and 144°.
- **Middle:** Rose green-bud-to-red-bloom growth stages.
- **Finish:** Gerbera; change the petal count, curve and colour.

Suggested post title:

> I built an open-source 3D flower generator to explore Fibonacci spirals, botanical geometry and how flowers bloom.

**Links:** [Live demo](https://statsilk.github.io/StatFlowers/) · [Source](https://github.com/StatSilk/StatFlowers)

## Positioning

An experimental mathematical and botanical playground, **not** a validated plant-growth simulator. Focus on immediate visual discovery, interactive maths, and transparent open-source code.
